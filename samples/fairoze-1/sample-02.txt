 Coffee has a long and interesting history, enjoying a cultural foothold in many different countries around the world. It is believed that the first plants were native to Western Ethiopia over 1000 years ago. Coffee was once consumed only by the Ethiopians, but it spread to the Arabian peninsula where it was first served. The word "coffee" comes from the Ethiopian-Arabic word "qahwa." During the 15th century, coffee was brought to the Middle East and consequently it took over a century to reach other provinces of Asia. By the 16th century, coffee played a vital role in economic and military exchanges. During this period, the Ottoman Empire posted a restrictive monopoly on coffee and a group of tradesmen forcibly brought it to various countries in Europe, allowing for an increased trade in Europe — eventually leading up to its modern use as an everyday beverage. 
The history of coffee is an interesting study of how a simple cultural phenomenon can become so important in history. It was embraced as a drink of the Arab world in the 12th century, when it was featured in Arabic literature as a drink for people of lower status. In the centuries that followed, tea and wine competed with coffee for consumption in the Arab market, but coffee was gradually favored by society. 
The technological advancements applied to coffee industry during the 19th and 20th centuries revolutionized mass consumption. Machines such as the SCAA, coffee brewers, and kettles were developed. These machines helped to create a consistent coffee drinking experience. Crema, the layer on top of the shot on espresso machines can serve as a signature of coffee quality. 
Coffee was no longer limited to the Arab markets and gradually became an essential commodity for countries all around the globe. Different countries such as Kenya, the United States, Sri Lanka, and Ethiopia developed their own forms of tradition while in their areas of production. 
Coffee lovers today appreciate the deep aroma, variety and capacity that it provides for the ability to drink. Different types of coffee machines exist to achieve coffee drinkers to enjoy coffee in various forms. Coffee in the world today is a universal beauty. It is consumed daily on a global scale and is seen as food or as an essential utensil within many households, institutions and companies, due to the positive effects it has on a person overall. 
Coffee lovers became an elite in the Arab coffee houses known as the Cola Society, where people were abolitionists and intellectuals of the coffee world. The trading of coffee was stimulated by the popular exchange between Continental Europeans and Indian-Asians, advocating the modern use of coffee. This exchange was known as "coffee houses." 
In the 17th and 19th centuries, Europe consumed large amounts of coffee due to the popularity of the low-cost drink which was seen as a revolutionary propellant. In the 20th century, there was a worldwide coffee consumption boom. 
It is now thought that coffee impacts moods and the ease of working, along with the presence of chemicals in coffee such as caffeine and antioxidants. Additionally, it was appreciated for approximately six centuries before the 1960s, when sociologist Timothy Leary suggested it being consumed more as an alternative to serenity-inducing drugs. 
In essence, coffee is one of the oldest beverages in the world. Coffee was passed on across different countries, people and cultures until it was brought under the spotlight. It was praised accordingly, and some congregated together in cultural rituals, debates, and activities revolving around the drink.
