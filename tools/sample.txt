To serve in pickleball, both feet have to stay behind the baseline until the paddle makes contact with the ball. Stepping on or over the line early, even just with your toes, is a fault. The serve itself must be underhand, struck below waist height, with the paddle head kept below the wrist at contact; unlike tennis, no overhead motion is allowed. Serves travel diagonally, cross-court, clearing the non-volley zone (the “kitchen”) entirely, since landing inside it is also a fault. Only one attempt is allowed per turn, and the receiving side lets the ball bounce once before returning it, the “two-bounce rule” that opens every rally.
